### Project 12: Hall Magnetic Sensor
![image170](media/27e8d67c5a1b3667cc29ff31ec8e8e0a.png)

**Description**

The KY-003 Hall Magnetic Sensor module acts as a switch that responds to the presence of a magnetic field by turning on or off.

It can detect magnetic materials within a range of up to 3cm.

The detection range is proportional to the magnetic field strength. The output is digital (on/off). It is compatible with popular microcontrollers such as Arduino, Raspberry Pi, and ESP32.

**How Does It Work?**

The Hall effect is a phenomenon related to the nature of current flow in a conductor. Current is composed of the movement of numerous small charge carriers, typically electrons, holes, ions (see Electromigration), or a combination of these.

When a magnetic field is present, these charges experience a force known as the Lorentz force. In the absence of such a magnetic field, charges generally follow approximately straight, 'line of sight' paths between collisions with impurities, phonons, etc.

However, when a magnetic field with a perpendicular component is applied, their paths between collisions become curved, causing moving charges to accumulate on one face of the material. This results in an equal and opposite charge exposure on the other face, where mobile charges are scarce.

The outcome is an asymmetric distribution of charge density across the Hall element, stemming from a force perpendicular to both the 'line of sight' path and the applied magnetic field. This charge separation establishes an electric field that counteracts further charge migration, thereby creating a steady electrical potential as long as the charge continues to flow.

![image171](media/7e5b94e30b3baa8717368c5bd08838be.jpeg)

This phenomenon is known as the Hall Effect.

Pinout

![image172](media/64d96ca7d3ed74d46b546a7b12a46ff2.png)

SIN is the Hall magnetic sensor signal pin, which we connect to the Arduino.

VCC should be connected to the +5V Power of Arduino.

GND should be connected to the ground of Arduino.

**Connection Diagram**

Connect the board's power line (middle) and ground (-) to +5V and GND on the Arduino, respectively. Connect the signal (S) to Pin 3 on the Arduino.

**Example Result**

Wire it up and upload the code to the board. You will observe that the D13 indicator on the PLUS board is off, and the LED on the module is also off.

However, if you bring a magnetic ball close to the Hall module, you will see the D13 indicator on the PLUS board turn on, and the LED on the module will also turn on.
